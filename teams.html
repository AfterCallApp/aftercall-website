The habit is the door

Logging the call builds the habit.
The habit opens the door.

Providers open AfterCall for themselves: to log their calls, track their skills and keep their certs current. Because they're already in the app after every run, and already journaling, the peer support your agency has in place is one tap from something they do anyway.

1

Log the call

Two minutes at the end of a run. Log it in 8 steps, typed, spoken, or pasted from CAD, and get a plain-language summary.

2

Check in and reflect

A voluntary post-call check-in on how the call landed, and a private journal for the ones that stay with you.

3

Reach your peer team

If the call warrants it, your trained peer supporters are already on the screen, reachable anonymously if a provider would rather.

Nothing changes about running the call. This is a journal, not a chart: no ePCR integration, no patient identifiers, PHI screening on every share.

For your peer support team

Who needs a check-in.
Never what they wrote.

A flag opens when a member marks a call high-impact, has a heavy run (4+ Serious or Critical calls, or 3+ Critical, within 24 hours), or taps "Ask for a check-in" after a rough call. Your peer supporters get a signal-only ping: who and why, never the call, never the content.

The part we will not negotiate

If your crews don't trust it,
none of the rest happens.

Adoption is the whole product, and adoption runs on trust. Every guarantee below is written into the agreement, not the brochure, and enforced in the database, not in policy.

The agency never sees

  • Any individual journal entry or reflection
  • The content of any peer support conversation
  • Direct messages between crew members
  • Any one person's wellness, stress, or sleep score
  • Who opened a peer support request

What is always true

  • Wellness reporting is aggregate-only, on every plan
  • Groups under five members are suppressed
  • Alerts carry the signal, never the content
  • A member who leaves keeps their own history
  • Members can leave the workspace at any time

AfterCall is not an ePCR and not a patient record system. It does not write into ESO, ImageTrend, or Zoll, and no patient identifier is retained: every share passes a five-layer PHI screen with a signed attestation. Nothing in AfterCall may be used in discipline, promotion, assignment, or a fitness-for-duty determination; we will sign that alongside your labor group. AfterCall is designed to keep PHI out and therefore a BAA is not currently necessary; SOC 2 Type II is on the roadmap and not in place today. We'd rather tell you exactly where each item stands than imply otherwise.

FAQ

Your questions, answered.

Does AfterCall replace our peer support team or EAP?

No. It makes them easier to reach. Your peer supporters, crisis line and EAP live right inside the app.

Why would providers actually use it?

Because they open it for themselves first: to log calls, track skills and keep certifications current. That habit is what puts your peer support one tap away after a hard call.

What does leadership actually see?

Anonymous team trends only. Groups under five are hidden automatically. You never see the person, the journal or the conversation.

Does it integrate with our ePCR?

No. AfterCall doesn't write into ESO, ImageTrend or Zoll, and no patient identifier is retained.

What does it cost?

Teams Essentials is $4.99 per seat per month and Teams Pro is $7.99 per seat per month. Command is quoted separately for 100+ seats.

Can we use grant money?

Many agencies can. Wellness programs qualify under FEMA AFG and several state and private grants.

Can AfterCall be used in discipline?

Never. Nothing in AfterCall may be used in discipline, promotion, assignment or fitness-for-duty decisions, and we'll put that in writing alongside your labor group.

What happens when a provider leaves the agency?

They keep their entire history: calls, journals, shifts and badges. The seat ends; their account stays theirs.
